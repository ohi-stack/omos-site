The OneGodian Algorithm™

The Algorithm is the evaluation and decision-logic layer used by OMOS. It does not replace the Protocol, O-H-I, or the runtime. The operational cycle is explicit and inspectable.

Observe

Capture the actual question, context, facts, constraints, evidence, source material, and uncertainty without prematurely deciding the answer.

Distill

Reduce non-material noise while preserving meaning. Layer 1 extracts objective, claims, questions, requirements, contradictions, evidence, ambiguity, and quarantined instructions.

Align

Evaluate candidate reasoning across explicit dimensions and hard gates instead of hiding judgment inside one opaque score.

Select

Compare alternatives, preserve dissent, identify evidence gaps, and select a defensible recommendation or escalate to human review.

Execute

Carry out only actions within the authorized scope. Consequential financial, legal, credential, registry, infrastructure, or external-system actions require separate authorization controls.

Verify

Check outcome, evidence, provenance, persistence, integrity, and whether the result actually satisfied the original objective. Verification is not the same as model agreement.

OMOS implementation
Customer runtime: Ask OMOS → Layer 1 → Alignment → Council Review → Governed Synthesis → Decision Record → Dashboard History.
Human Gate: approval/rejection is enforced in the governed record transition and does not automatically authorize unrelated external actions.
Alignment state: Truth, Clarity, Coherence, Dignity, Constructive Unity, Evidence Support, Verifiability, Transparency, Reproducibility, Security, Long-Term Benefit, and Execution Readiness can be represented independently.
Hard gates: dignity, consent, authority, fabricated evidence, instruction integrity, and unresolved high risk may block or escalate even when weighted scores are otherwise high.
Record discipline: inputs, versions, provider provenance, hashes, timestamps, verification state, and human disposition belong in the Decision Record.
Operational standard: deterministic logic can make a process reproducible, but reproducibility does not guarantee factual truth. Evidence and verification remain separate from scoring.
