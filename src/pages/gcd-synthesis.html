OHI • GCD Synthesis • Common Ground + Remainders

Find the strongest shared structure without erasing disagreement.

GCD Synthesis is an OMOS/OHI repeated-reduction method inspired conceptually by the mathematical greatest common divisor. It does not perform arithmetic on model answers and it does not make convergence equal truth. Its purpose is to identify defensible shared structure while preserving provider-specific remainders that could change a decision.

Repeated reduction

For independent outputs A, B, C, and D, OMOS conceptually reduces them in sequence: G1 = GCD(A,B) → G2 = GCD(G1,C) → G3 = GCD(G2,D). At each step the practical question is: what remains defensible after comparing these perspectives?

Common ground and remainders

Each contribution can be represented conceptually as Oᵢ = G + Rᵢ, where G is the shared structure and Rᵢ is that contributor's material remainder. OMOS should preserve remainders involving risk, dissent, assumptions, evidence gaps, or alternative conclusions rather than deleting them to make the result look unanimous.

Contradictions are first-class records

A material contradiction should identify the disputed claim, supporting position, challenging position, evidence required, and resolution state. A contradiction that cannot be verified remains unresolved.

Evidence outranks consensus

Evidence → Verified State → Reasoned Consensus → Confidence. Four models agreeing can be useful comparative evidence, but four models can also share the same mistaken premise. Verification therefore happens after common-ground extraction.

Governed synthesis

A useful conceptual form is S = G + R(material) + E + V: shared structure, material dissent, evidence, and verification state. The resulting synthesis then goes to the Human Gate rather than declaring itself final authority.

Complete logic

Inputs → Normalize → Decompose → Compare → Extract Common Ground → Preserve Remainders → Detect Contradictions → Weight Evidence → Verify → Synthesize → Human Decision.
